Time to Surface

Technical dive planner & decompression calculator for iPhone

Download on the App Store

Free. No in-app purchases, no subscription, no account.

Staged decompression from 15 to 51 metres — deeper to 56 with a warning — on six bottom gases, with one deco bottle. Runtime, stops, minimum gas and both cylinder fills, before you kit up.

What it plans

  • Staged decompression, 15 m to 51 m. Past 51 m it plans and warns rather than refuses — gas density binds before oxygen does there.
  • Six bottom gases — EAN32, triox 30/30, EAN28, air, 21/35 and 18/45.
  • One decompression gas — EAN50 or oxygen — in an AL80, AL50 or AL40, on a twinset back gas you pick.
  • Bühlmann ZH-L16C, gradient factors yours to set, 20/85 by default.
  • ppO2 1.2 bar working, 1.6 bar at a stop by default — stricter than the 1.4 most divers trained at. Both adjustable.
  • Descent and ascent rates you set — 10 m/min down, 9 m/min to the first stop, 3 m/min between stops, and the last 6 m at 1 m/min.
  • Minimum gas, the workbook's C × A × T: what two divers on one back-gas supply need to leave the deepest point and reach the surface, at the minimum ascent rate rather than the planned one.
  • Deco bottle fill and reserve, walked level by level from the schedule that was actually solved, not estimated.
  • CNS, and which of back gas, deco bottle or CNS ends the dive.
  • Repetitive dives — keep a plan under a name, then say up to three came before this one, each with its surface interval. Nitrogen and helium both cross the interval; CNS decays at a 90-minute half-time.
  • Contingencies, re-solved — lose the deco bottle, add five minutes, or go three metres deeper, each run through the same model rather than scaled by a rule of thumb.
  • Pragmatic decompression, off by default — a simplified profile for the water, not a more conservative one.
  • A wetnote page, one tap, sized to copy onto your slate by hand.
  • Metric or imperial.

What it does not plan

Closed-circuit rebreathers. Cave or wreck penetration. Hypoxic mixes and travel gas. More than one decompression gas. Altitude — sea level only. It does not compute OTU.

It refuses rather than warns

Inside its envelope: a gas past its oxygen limit, an exposure past the maximum, cylinders that will not cover the plan, a surface interval under an hour — all produce no numbers at all, rather than a number with a caution attached.

Your dive computer owns the dive

This is pre-dive arithmetic. It never pronounces a dive safe, and it is not a substitute for training, for your computer, or for your own judgement in the water.
